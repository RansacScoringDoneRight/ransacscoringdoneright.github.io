Experiments

Reproducing the paper

The paper's figures are drawn by drivers in the research project neurips2026/, which has its own environment:

cd neurips2026
julia --project=. -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.instantiate()'
julia --project=. scripts/fetch_data.jl          # a machine without the data (neurips2026/data/README.md)
julia --project=. scripts/fig_profile.jl --plan
julia --project=. test/runtests.jl
make -C paper

neurips2026/REPRODUCE.md maps every figure of the paper to its driver, its config of record and its data. The figures regenerate byte for byte from the six stored evaluation runs of record. The evaluation that produced those runs is documented there but not yet ported to the current stack.

Scoring studies (retired)

Two scoring studies and a decision probe used to ship beside the package as drivers in neurips2026/scripts/studies/. They were retired in 0.8.3 and are not reproducible from VisualGeometryToolkitEval 0.10 on: their pool of record (PT-E-SIFT-4K) is a pool format that VisualGeometryToolkitEval no longer reads. Their write-ups stay in neurips2026/notes/ as records of the runs they describe, and the drivers and configs are in the repository's history up to tag v0.8.2. Both studies exercised the selection rule alone, with no local optimization: every scorer scored the same stored minimal-sample hypotheses of the PhotoTourism essential-matrix pool, and every scorer was called from the package itself.

These are not the paper's Profile and Sensitivity experiments, whose figure drivers are neurips2026/scripts/fig_profile.jl and fig_sensitivity.jl (see the Overview and neurips2026/REPRODUCE.md).

Environment

neurips2026/ is its own Julia project, not a dependency of the package: the drivers need VisualGeometryToolkitEval (pool IO, the run records), which the package deliberately does not depend on, and the concrete problem types of VisualGeometryToolkitEstimation, which the package itself uses only for SIMFIT++.

cd neurips2026 && julia --project=. -e 'using Pkg; Pkg.instantiate()'

Sample budget under scale miscalibration (the write-up)

Each scorer's operating point is read from the deployed cross-validation sidecar, tuned at calibration (ζ = 1) and not re-tuned; miscalibration multiplies every scorer's native width by the same ζ, which is principled for both families because both natives are linear in the noise scale. The budget is counted in minimal samples, from 100 to 4 000, on 8 800 pairs of four held-out test scenes.

The write-up reports mAA@10° and median pose error at every budget for the Jeffreys score, MSAC, GaU, RANSAC and MAGSAC++. Calibrated, the five are within 0.013 mAA of one another at 4 000 samples; at ζ = 32 the Jeffreys score retains 83 % of its calibrated mAA and keeps improving with the budget, while the four baselines retain 16–23 % and get worse as the budget grows.

Per-hypothesis scoring cost (the write-up)

Single-threaded, on the stored residuals of one PhotoTourism scene, every scorer at one shared operating point; the figure is the minimum over three repetitions, per hypothesis scored.

The sort-and-sweep costs more than RANSAC and MSAC and less than GaU and closed-form MAGSAC++; its cost grows linearly with the number of points, since both its radix sort and its sweep are linear. The write-up's MAGSAC++ lookup-table row was measured with a tabulated scorer that has since been removed from the package; the current driver times the exact forms only.
